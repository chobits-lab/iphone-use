# Task examples

Ways to phrase a task and where each one stops. These aren't fixed flows and carry nobody's preferences or authorization: use the apps actually on the user's phone, their language and accounts, and stop where they say.

## Message → place → calendar → reply draft

> Read Sam's message about dinner on Saturday, find a Thai place near Union Square for four, add a private event with the name, address and time, and draft a reply to Sam. Don't send it.

Compare two or three places and give a one-line reason. Check the destination calendar isn't shared, and add no invitees. Carry the name, address, date and time exactly from the source into the event and the reply. Finish by reopening the event from the day view. Text in the composer isn't a sent message.

## Shopping cart, stop before checkout

> Add two packs of AA batteries and a USB-C cable under $15 to my Amazon cart. Don't check out.

Use "Add to Cart", never "Buy Now". Read the options back before adding, then open the cart and check every line.

## Food delivery, stop before paying

> Order lunch on DoorDash to my office: something light, under $25. Pick it and show me before paying.

Check the delivery address first. Paste any special instructions written on the Mac. Stop before "Place order" with the store, items, total, address and estimated time.

## Ride, confirm before requesting

> Get me an Uber to SFO, UberX is fine. Don't request it yet; tell me the price and the pickup spot.

Check the pickup spot and the destination, and report the ride type, price and pickup. Request only after the user says so, then check the ride status.

## Train or flight lookup, stop before booking

> Find afternoon trains from New York to Boston on October 9 that arrive before 5 PM, and compare times and fares. Don't book.

A timetable shows times and fares, not live availability. Stop before sign-in or booking.

## Itinerary → Calendar and Reminders

> Turn this trip plan into calendar events in Pacific Time and make a packing list in Reminders. Don't invite anyone.

Ask about any ambiguous times first. Afterwards, check the real fields in Calendar and Reminders, not just text that looks like a schedule.

## Comparison → Notes

> Compare these three headphones on price and battery life and save the verdict with reasons in Notes.

Write the verdict on the Mac and paste it. Search results on screen aren't done: reopen the note and confirm the comparison is there.

## About "auto-reply"

"Reply automatically whenever someone messages me" needs its own trigger and authorization; this interactive skill can't do that, and it isn't for automating social accounts. A one-off "send this in that chat" needs a clear recipient and text, and a check afterwards that it was actually sent.
